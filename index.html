About

I am a researcher in fluvial and coastal ecohydraulics, working at the intersection of hydrodynamics, sediment transport, and living systems. My research combines controlled flume experiments, field observation, and computational modelling to understand the coupled physics that governs how rivers and coasts erode, build, and respond to change.

My doctoral work (IIT Guwahati) examined turbulence and sediment transport in alluvial channels. My postdoctoral research at the University of Central Florida and at Scripps Institution of Oceanography extended this foundation to living coasts — mangroves, oyster reefs, living shorelines, and tidal inlets.

At IISc, my group focuses on nature-based coastal protection for India, bringing engineering rigour to the design and evaluation of living shorelines and reef–vegetation systems.

Research

My program studies the same flow–sediment–ecosystem coupling across the river-to-coast continuum, approached through three linked domains.

Physics

How flow, turbulence, and sediment transport respond to vegetation, reefs, and evolving bed and shoreline morphology — and the thresholds that separate stability from erosion.

Application

Translating mechanistic understanding into predictive models and nature-based solutions for erosion, flood mitigation, and shoreline protection along India's coast.

Design & Methods

Designing and evaluating living shorelines and reef–vegetation systems, using flume experiments, field observation, and computational modelling as one integrated loop.
